# 01 — Product Vision and Core Game Loop
**Owner:** Overall design  
**Status:** Core concept LOCKED; timing and tuning PROPOSED

## Premise
A player commands a single adventurer undertaking self-directed expeditions through a large, interconnected dark-fantasy or other original ruined world. The player supplies a kit, itinerary, route preference, combat doctrine and risk limits. The adventurer handles walking, routine interaction, threat response and fighting autonomously. Players retain limited but important authority: redirect at safe decision points, make rare major advancement choices and order retreat at any time.

The player's verbs are **prepare, plan, dispatch, observe, intervene, extract, learn, adapt**. Not attack or dodge. Analogies to other works are illustrative only; do not copy their world, enemies, art, naming or progression.

## Product promise
A short session generates a legible little story: a mission was planned, a route revealed, the adventurer adapted to trouble, something was gained, and a decision either protected or jeopardised the haul. The next expedition benefits from improved world knowledge, even after a death.

## Design pillars
1. **Planning beats twitch reflexes.** Route choice and orders meaningfully affect expected outcome.
2. **The world remembers.** Terrain revealed, shortcuts opened, major events and defeated bosses are persistent.
3. **Retreat is gameplay.** Ordering home starts an actual return journey; banking happens only at camp.
4. **Autonomy is trustworthy.** The character obeys clear priorities, makes reasonable local choices and logs their causes.
5. **Risk is knowable, never fully certain.** Fixed regional identity plus variable encounters prevents perfect memorisation without nullifying knowledge.
6. **Finite campaign, optional mastery.** Bosses can be permanently defeated and world completion has an ending; rematches and challenge runs are optional.
7. **Readable at a glance.** Mobile-size interaction and spectator comprehension matter as much as RPG depth.

## Macro loop
1. **Camp:** select starter equipment (prototype: sword and shield only), set expedition objectives, doctrine and survival thresholds; inspect mapped world and death cache.
2. **Dispatch:** adventurer follows a plan into accessible regions, automatically navigates and resolves routine encounters.
3. **Opportunity:** fights and exploration grant transient power, loot, knowledge and new route options; significant sanctuary or discovery prompts may request the player's choice.
4. **Decision:** continue for higher rewards, divert, attempt boss, or call retreat.
5. **Outcome:** physically reach camp to bank rewards; die and leave a recoverable cache; or defeat a boss, altering world state immediately, while still needing extraction for loot.
6. **Reflection:** the Chronicle shows route, pivotal decisions, items and cause of death; player adjusts next plan.

## Outcomes are intentionally independent
- **Extraction success:** adventurer physically arrives at camp alive; eligible carried valuables and recoverable mastery are secured.
- **Major world victory:** boss killed or shortcut permanently activated; persists even if carrier subsequently dies.
- **Partial gain from failure:** exploration intel and permanent world changes survive death; carried assets do not bank.
- **Recovery attempt:** next run can target the marked death cache; retrieval is not equivalent to extraction.

## Target sessions (PROPOSED)
Short 3–5 minutes, normal 8–12, ambitious 15–25. These are design hypotheses to validate, not runtime guarantees. Speed controls for known, safe traversal can reduce passive dead time. Never force compulsory idle waits to improve monetisation metrics.

## Audience and positioning
Suitable for players who enjoy map mastery, auto-combat observation, extraction decisions and repeatable low-input sessions. Distinct from action RPGs through delegated moment-to-moment execution; distinct from idle RPGs through risky physical routes and consequential planning; distinct from procedural roguelikes through fixed persistent geography.

## Success and failure signals
- Players intentionally alter their route or orders following a failed or successful expedition.
- A typical death has a traceable chain of cause and effect, not an opaque statistical surprise.
- A shortcut changes which return path the player chooses.
- Watching the adventurer is interesting during encounters, but safely traversed ground can be accelerated.
- A player can reach a meaningful session conclusion without babysitting every routine enemy.

## Exclusions for the first playable build
No multiple party members, co-op, PvP, free-form AI scripting, base construction, massive crafting trees, distinct classes, live-service power creep, procedural world replacement or direct combat controls. Future revenue mechanics are not part of the vertical-slice success criteria.
